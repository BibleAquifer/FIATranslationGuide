## 2 Chronicles 1:1–6 (id: eng-2ch-p1-v1)

Hear and Heart
--------------

In this step, hear 2 Chronicles 1:1–6 and put it in your hearts.

Listen to an audio version of 2 Chronicles 1:1–6 three times, in three different translations, if possible.

Pause this audio here.

Now, as a group, discuss the following questions:

1. What do you like in this passage? Pause this audio here.
2. What do you not like or not understand in this passage? Pause this audio here.
3. What does this passage tell us about God? Pause this audio here.
4. What does this passage tell us about people? Pause this audio here.
5. How does this passage affect our daily lives? Pause this audio here.
6. Who do you know who needs to hear this passage?

Setting the Stage
-----------------

Listen to an audio version of 2 Chronicles 1:1–6 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

Solomon, the son of King David, became the new king of Israel after David died. This story happens soon after David died.

Solomon made himself strong as king and took full control of the kingdom of Israel. King Solomon made himself strong by defeating his enemies to become the only ruler of Israel. King Solomon's older brother, Adonijah, had tried to become king, but Solomon told Solomon's military leader, Benaiah, to kill Adonijah. Solomon also told Benaiah to kill Joab and Shimei. Joab had been King David's military leader and had supported Adonijah when Adonijah rebelled. Shimei was from the tribe of King Saul and had cursed King David when David ran away from Absalom, because Shimei hated David and blamed David for the death of King Saul's sons. Solomon had also banished Abiathar the priest from the priesthood. These men had all been enemies of King David and were dangerous to Solomon now as king. Solomon also began to build a wall around Jerusalem and likely made sure the army stayed loyal to Solomon.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Talk about a person who became the leader of your community or country after that person struggled with other people who also wanted to become the leader. Describe all the things that the new leader did after he became the leader to make sure that he remained the leader and became strong. Pause this audio here.

Solomon became strong because Yahweh, Solomon's God, was with Solomon. Yahweh was the true king of Israel and had chosen Solomon to rule. Yahweh blessed Solomon, protected Solomon, and helped Solomon take full control of the kingdom. Yahweh made Solomon a great and powerful king. Yahweh also gave Solomon wealth and honor during Solomon's rule.

After Solomon took control of the kingdom of Israel, Solomon spoke to the people of Israel. King Solomon called all the people of Israel and their leaders together. It was normal for a king to gather the people for important events. The storyteller lists the leaders to show that all of Israel supported Solomon. Solomon spoke to the army commanders, the judges, and the leaders of Israel who were heads of clans, or groups of families and tribes. The army commanders who led the military of Israel were in charge of groups of 100 and 1,000 soldiers.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Discuss how you organize your warriors and the different levels of people who lead the warriors. Tell of a time when these leaders of the warriors had a disagreement with the great chief of your people. Pause this audio here.

The judges were also leaders in Israel because they were responsible for making sure people followed the rules. The judges did this by listening to the people's problems and deciding what was right according to the law of Israel. The heads of families were wise and important men from each clan or large family group.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Discuss what your community's leadership structure looks like. For example, you may say that fathers lead their homes, clan leaders lead the clans, and the chiefs lead the community. Tell about a time when all these leaders, as well as the people, were gathered together. Pause this audio here.

We do not know what Solomon said in the gathering, but we know what happened next. Solomon and all the people went to the high place at Gibeon. High places were special places where people could worship, with altars for sacrifices. Before the Israelites built the temple, people used high places to worship Yahweh. People went to the high place at Gibeon because the Tent of Meeting that Moses had made was there. Yahweh had told Moses that Yahweh would meet the people there and that the people should bring their sacrifices to the tent.

Stop here as a group and look at a picture of the Tent of Meeting. Talk about the place where the people of your community believe they can meet with God, the spirits, or the ancestors. Tell about a time that the leaders of your community went to that place, what they did, and why they did it. Pause this audio here.

The storyteller tells us that the ark of God was not in the Tent of Meeting at Gibeon. The ark was not there because King David had moved the ark from Kiriath Jearim to Jerusalem and placed it in a new tent. Normally, the ark stayed in the Tent of Meeting, but the Philistines had taken the ark of God during a battle. Later, the Philistines returned the ark, and then the people of Beth Shemesh took it and gave it to the people in Kiriath Jearim, where it stayed for 20 years. Then David brought the ark to Jerusalem. Even though the ark of God was not at Gibeon, the bronze altar was still there. This is the same altar that Yahweh had instructed Moses to make for burning sacrifices. Bezalel, the grandson of Hur, is the one who made this altar with the wisdom that Yahweh had given Bezalel.

Stop here as a group and look at a picture of the ark of God and the bronze altar. Pause this audio here.

Solomon and the people worshiped Yahweh at the bronze altar. Solomon offered 1,000 burnt offerings. The priests likely killed the animals, cut them up, and burned them completely, except for the skin, which they kept. These offerings showed that the people wanted to dedicate themselves fully to Yahweh. It is likely that the people dedicated their new king, Solomon, to Yahweh.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Describe the kind of offerings that the people of your community offer God, the spirits, or ancestors to please them. Tell of a time when a leader has offered a large number of sacrifices. Why did the leader do this?

Defining the Scenes
-------------------

Listen to an audio version of 2 Chronicles 1:1–6 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In this step, the group will define the scenes, characters, and setting of this passage. Then the group will visualize this passage.

This passage has four scenes.

**In the first scene:** King Solomon took firm control of the kingdom of Israel because Yahweh was with Solomon. Yahweh made Solomon very great.

**In the second scene:** King Solomon spoke to all the people of Israel and to their military leaders, judges, and the heads of the families of Israel.

**In the third scene:** King Solomon and all the people who had gathered went to the high place at Gibeon because the Tent of Meeting that Moses had made was there. The bronze altar that Bezalel, the son of Uri, had made was also there.

**In the fourth scene:** King Solomon and the people of Israel worshipped Yahweh there. And King Solomon offered 1,000 burnt offerings.

The characters in this passage are:

* Yahweh
* King Solomon
* King David
* All the people of Israel
* The military commanders of thousands
* The military commanders of hundreds
* The judges of Israel
* The leading men of Israel who are heads of the families, or clans
* Moses
* Bezalel, the son of Uri and grandson of Hur
* And the priest who offered Solomon's sacrifices—the storyteller does not mention the priests, but they were likely there

As a group, pay attention to these parts of the passage's setting:

King David had died, and David's son Solomon had become the new king of Israel. It is not clear how long after King David died this story takes place, but it is not too long. In the first scene, King Solomon strengthened himself over his kingdom, or took firm control of Israel. A king is normally the most powerful man in the kingdom. The king rules with the help of other powerful people, who can be dangerous to the king's rule if they are not loyal to the king. King Solomon strengthened himself over his kingdom by making sure that there was no one to challenge Solomon's rule. Solomon also strengthened himself over Israel by doing things that would make the Kingdom of Israel safe from its enemies. You will remember from the story in 1 Kings that Solomon built the wall of Jerusalem and made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Talk about a leader who ruled your community or country strongly. Talk about how the leader was in control of everything. List the things that the leader did to make himself strong from the day he first became the leader. Pay attention to the words and phrases you use to describe the way the leader made himself strong and took full control. Pause this audio here.

Although Solomon made himself strong over Israel, Solomon was only able to do so because Yahweh, Solomon's God, was with Solomon. When a king was successful, the Israelites said that it was because Yahweh was with that king. Yahweh was the true king of Israel, and Yahweh chose which man would rule as Yahweh's representative. Yahweh had chosen Solomon, and therefore Yahweh protected Solomon and made him able to take control of the kingdom of Israel. Yahweh made Solomon very great. Yahweh had promised that Yahweh would make Solomon great. It is possible that the storyteller is referring to how Yahweh made Solomon great and gave him power and honor throughout Solomon's rule, not just at the beginning of Solomon's rule.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Talk about a person who was very successful. Tell about how God, the spirits, or ancestors protected that person, blessed him, and helped him succeed. What special language would you use to say that God, the spirits, or the ancestors made that person able to become successful? Pause this audio here.

In the second scene, King Solomon spoke to all Israel. It is likely that King Solomon called a great gathering, or assembly, of all the people of Israel. It was common for kings to call for such gatherings at important times, especially at the beginning of the king's rule. The storyteller focuses on the levels of leaders who were there at the gathering. The storyteller likely does this to show that the people of Israel were united and loyal to King Solomon. King Solomon spoke to the leaders of Israel's military. Israel's army had fighting units made up of 1,000 soldiers each. Each unit of 1,000 soldiers had its leader. There were many of these units. Their leaders are the commanders of thousands. The military units of 1,000 were further broken down into units of 100 soldiers, each with its leader. These are the commanders of hundreds. King Solomon also spoke to the judges of Israel. The people often called rulers or leaders of Israel "judges." In this story, it is likely that the judges that King Solomon spoke to are the judges who decided which person was right or wrong when two people or groups had an argument. King Solomon also spoke to all the leading men of Israel who were heads of families. You will remember that there were 12 tribes in Israel, and each tribe was made up of clans or large family groups. From the days of Moses, the Israelites had tribal leaders as well as leaders of clans or large family groups.

Stop here and do this activity as a group: Act out a scene where a military commander has harmed a person or their property. The person whom the commander harmed or his family should go to their clan leader and explain what happened and ask for justice. Their clan leader should then take them before a judge to ask for justice. Pay attention to the words and phrases that you use for military commander, clan leader, and judge. Pause this audio here.

In the third scene, King Solomon and all the people who had gathered went to the high place that was at Gibeon. A high place usually refers to a dedicated place of worship set up on a high place, like a hill or mountain. But many of the high places of Israel were not on hills or mountains. It is better to think of a high place as a shrine or a sacred place of worship.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Describe a physical place that your community or another community you know goes to offer sacrifices. Talk about what the place looks like and how important it is. Pay attention to the words and phrases you use when you name this place. Make sure you choose a general name that you can also use to describe a similar worship place that belongs to another group of people. Pause this audio here.

King Solomon and the people went to the high place that was at Gibeon because the Tent of Meeting for God was there. Gibeon was one of the cities of the Levites, in the territory of Benjamin. Gibeon was about 10 kilometers northwest of Jerusalem.

Stop here and look at a map of the journey from Jerusalem to Gibeon. Pause this audio here.

This is the same tent that Moses, who served Yahweh faithfully, had made in the wilderness. Yahweh had asked Moses to make a temporary house that people made of skins. Yahweh said that Yahweh would speak with the people of Israel from the Tent of Meeting. The Israelites, therefore, called it the Tent of Meeting, the tabernacle, or the tent of Yahweh's presence. The storyteller adds a comment to explain to us that the ark of God was not in the Tent of Meeting like it was when the Israelites first made the tent. Earlier, King David had taken the ark from Kiriath Jearim to Jerusalem and put it in another tent at the place King David had prepared for it.

Stop here and do this activity as a group: Have someone tell a story. As the person is telling the story, he should pause the story to explain or add some information about what he has been saying. For example, the person could be speaking of the story of a great harvest that your community has enjoyed and then pause the story to make a comment about someone who did not plant anything at planting time. Pause this audio here.

But the bronze altar that Bezalel, the son of Uri and grandson of Hur, had made was there standing in front of the Tent of Meeting. Bezalel had made this altar by mixing two metals, copper and tin, to make a metal called bronze. The altar was standing before the Tent of Meeting because that is what Yahweh had commanded. Sometimes the word tabernacle refers to the entire Tent of Meeting, including the fence around it. In this case, it refers only to the two rooms in the main tent, the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place.

Stop here and, as a group, look at a picture of the entire Tent of Meeting with the bronze altar standing before the Tent of Meeting. Pause audio here.

In the fourth scene, King Solomon and the people with him worshipped or asked Yahweh for advice there. It is possible that the storyteller means that Solomon and the people went to sacrifice at Gibeon often. King Solomon went up to the bronze altar in front of Yahweh's Tent of Meeting and offered 1,000 burnt offerings on the altar. It is unlikely that Solomon himself made the offerings. Normally, priests would kill, cut up, and burn the entire animal on the altar, apart from its skin. It is likely that Solomon offered the burnt offerings to dedicate himself and his rule over Israel to Yahweh.

Now, the group should storyboard, draw, or use objects to visualize the passage and the action in it.

Filling the Gaps

Listen to an audio version of 2 Chronicles 1:1–6 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In this step, the group will discuss key terms and words in this passage.

Solomon, the son of David, established himself in his kingdom, and Yahweh, Solomon's God, was with Solomon. A kingdom is the territory or the people that a king rules. Yahweh is a name that God gives himself. This name means something like "I am," or "I am who I am." God is a less personal or more general name that people sometimes used to refer to Yahweh. This is the God who created everything, the most powerful spiritual being.

Stop here and discuss as a group what words or phrases you will use for **kingdom**, **Yahweh**, and **God**. Look up kingdom, Yahweh, and God in the Master Glossary for more information. If you have already translated these words in another book of the Bible, use the same words that you have used there. Pause this audio here.

Solomon spoke to all Israel, to the commanders, judges, leaders, and heads of families. Israel is the land that Yahweh promised to Abraham and his descendants. At some point, the people of Israel fought among themselves and divided the land into two kingdoms. At this point, Israel refers to the whole kingdom before it was divided. A commander is the military officer who is in charge of other soldiers. Israel's military commanders were in charge of either 1,000 or 100 soldiers. A judge is a person who judges actions that people do and decides if what those people have done is right or wrong, and therefore if a person is guilty or innocent of breaking a law. A judge will punish the person if the judge decides that the person is guilty of doing wrong. The leaders were the chiefs or leading men in all Israel. The heads of families were specifically the chiefs or the most important men from each clan, or large family group, in Israel. It is likely that leaders and heads of families refer to the same group of men.

Stop here and discuss as a group what words or phrases you will use for **Israel**, **commanders**, **judges**, **leaders**, and **heads**. Look up Israel and judges in the Master Glossary for more information. If you have already translated these words in another book of the Bible, use the same words that you have used there. Pause this audio here.

Solomon and all the assembly went to the high place at Gibeon because the Tent of Meeting that Moses, the faithful or loyal servant of Yahweh, had made in the wilderness was there. "All the assembly" refers to all the people who had gathered together for a common purpose. Because Yahweh chose the people of Israel, people usually call gatherings of the people of Israel the "assembly" of Yahweh. The Tent of Meeting is another name for the tabernacle. This is where the Israelites worshipped Yahweh. The people called it the Tent of Meeting because it was where the Israelites could meet Yahweh. A servant is a person who does work for someone else. People sometimes call someone who worships and obeys Yahweh a servant of Yahweh. A wilderness is a lonely, barren place without water. There are almost no trees or bushes growing in a wilderness. The ground is dry and rocky, and there are wild animals roaming around. People do not live in the wilderness.

Stop here and discuss as a group what words or phrases you will use for **assembly**, **Tent of Meeting**, **servant**, and **wilderness**. Look up **tabernacle**, servant, and wilderness in the Master Glossary for more information. If you have already translated these words in another book of the Bible, use the same word that you have used there. Pause this audio here.

But David had brought the ark of God to Jerusalem. The bronze altar that Bezalel, the son of Uri, had made was also there in front of the tabernacle. The ark of God is the oblong box that people made of acacia wood that Yahweh asked Moses to make. Sometimes people call this box the ark of the covenant. Yahweh spoke to Moses from above the ark. Bronze is a metal that people made by combining copper and tin. An altar is a place where people make offerings or sacrifices to God. The people of Israel burned their animal sacrifices on the bronze altar. The tabernacle was a portable temple that the Israelites carried with them during their years of traveling in the wilderness. This was the place where the Israelites kept the ark of the covenant and where the people worshiped God.

Stop here and discuss as a group what words or phrases you will use for **ark**, **bronze**, **altar**, and **tabernacle**. Look up **ark of the covenant**, bronze, and altar in the Master Glossary for more information. Remember that the storyteller calls the ark the "ark of God" in this story. Remember that "tabernacle" is another way to say the Tent of Meeting. If you have already translated these words in another book of the Bible, use the same word that you have used there. Pause this audio here.

Solomon offered 1,000 burnt offerings on the bronze altar. A burnt offering is a form of sacrifice that people bring to Yahweh. When a person brings Yahweh a burnt offering, he kills an animal and burns this animal completely on an altar. The people brought Yahweh burnt offerings as a way to completely dedicate themselves to Yahweh.

Stop here and discuss as a group what word or phrase you will use for **burnt offering**. Look up burnt offering in the Master Glossary for more information. If you have already translated this word in another book of the Bible, use the same word that you have used there.

Setting the Stage

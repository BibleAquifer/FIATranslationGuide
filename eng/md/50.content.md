Setting the Stage
-----------------

Listen to an audio version of Philippians 1:1—11 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

Paul is in prison in Rome in the early 60s A.D. Paul is in prison because Paul has been preaching the gospel, or good news, about Jesus. As a Roman citizen, Paul has appealed to Caesar, the leader, or "emperor," of the Roman Empire, to judge Paul's case at the highest level, and Paul waits to hear what Caesar will decide.

Stop here and look at the map. Find Rome as a group. Pause this audio here.

Nobody knows for sure if the authorities will release Paul and give him freedom to preach about Jesus, or if Paul will stay in prison and maybe even receive the death penalty. So Paul writes this letter to comfort Paul's dear friends in the city of Philippi. Philippi was a Roman city in eastern Macedonia, north of the sea, in a plain, a flat area of land.

Stop here and look at the map. Find Macedonia and Philippi as a group. Pause this audio here.

Paul was a teacher and friend to a young man named Timothy, who often traveled with Paul to share the good news about Jesus. Timothy is with Paul now, and even though the letter is mainly from Paul, Paul says the letter is coming from both Paul and Timothy. Timothy may be visiting Paul while Paul is in prison, or Timothy may be regularly helping Paul in Paul's ministry from prison.

Paul begins his letter by greeting all of God's holy people in Philippi who belong to Christ Jesus, including the church leaders and deacons. The deacons are people in the church who probably help the church leaders. The deacons may help by handling the money, baptizing people, serving people, and doing other things the church leaders need help with. This means that Paul is writing the letter to the people in the church in Philippi, and Paul is probably expecting everyone there who believes in Jesus to be at church and to listen to the letter together.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Give an example of someone in your culture who sends a message to someone else. How does the sender describe themselves? How does the sender describe the person or people they address the message to? Pause this audio here.

In most of the letters that Paul wrote, Paul calls himself an apostle, or someone whom God sends out with God's authority to tell the good news about Jesus. But Paul does not need to remind the Christians in Philippi of Paul's authority. The Christians in Philippi had a close relationship with Paul, and they partnered with Paul to share the gospel. The people in the church gave Paul money to pay for the cost of travel and to share with the poor people that Paul met. So Paul does not call himself an apostle in this letter. Instead, Paul calls himself and Timothy slaves of Jesus Christ.

Immediately after Paul gives the standard greeting to the people to whom Paul writes the letter, Paul talks about how grateful Paul is for the Philippians, how Paul has been praying for the Philippians, and how much Paul loves the Philippians. Paul wants the Philippians to focus on God and what God has done for the Philippians.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: Can you think of a time when there was a problem that distracted you from what was more important? Give examples.

Defining the Scenes
-------------------

Listen to an audio version of Philippians 1:1—11 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In this step, the group will define the parts, characters, and setting of this passage. Then the group will visualize this passage.

This passage has three parts.

**In the first part:** Paul and Timothy say they are servants of Jesus and that this message is from Paul and Timothy. Paul and Timothy also say that this message is for the Christians in Philippi.

**In the second part:** Paul tells the Philippians that Paul has been praying for them. Paul also says how much Paul loves the Philippians and is thankful for them.

**In the third part:** Paul prays for the Christians in Philippi to know God even better than they already do. Paul wants the Christians in Philippi to live for God and to do the right things.

The characters in this passage are:

* Paul
* Timothy
* God our Father
* Christ Jesus
* The Christians in Philippi
* And someone who reads the letter out loud, probably Epaphroditus

As a group, pay attention to these parts of the passage's setting:

Paul and Timothy are in Rome. The Philippians are in Philippi, where they live. Epaphroditus travels from Rome to Philippi to deliver this message from Paul and Timothy to the Philippians in the church in Philippi.

In the first part of the message, Paul and Timothy greet the Philippians.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: How do you greet people in your community? What special words or phrases do you use to greet people? What greeting do you use when you send a message to someone you know and love? Give examples. Pause this audio here.

Remember that Paul and the Philippians are all close friends, and Paul has not seen the Philippians in a long time. Paul misses the Philippians, and the Philippians miss Paul. The Philippians are worried about the fact that Paul is in prison and that Paul may receive the death penalty. Paul is also sad that people are persecuting the Philippians because the Philippians follow Jesus. Paul wants to encourage and comfort the Philippians with the truth and remind the Philippians of what Jesus has done for them.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: How do you feel when you have not seen someone you love in a long time? What would you want to say to that person? Pause this audio here.

Paul calls the believers in Philippi God's holy people, or saints. Holy people are people whom God has set apart especially to serve God. These holy people are "in" Christ Jesus. In other words, these holy people know Jesus—they serve Jesus and constantly talk with Jesus and learn from Jesus. In Paul's message to the church, Paul includes the church leaders, or overseers, and the church deacons. You will remember that the overseer of a church was the leader of the church, and the deacons helped the overseer. Paul wants everyone to hear this letter from Paul.

Stop here and discuss as a group what you call the different leaders in your church. What do you call the lead pastor or pastors? What do you call the helpers, or the deacons? Pause this audio here.

Paul wishes "grace and peace" on the believers in Philippi. "Grace and peace" was a common Christian greeting during that time, and people said this greeting to each other because they wanted each other to remember that it is God and Christ Jesus who showed grace to us, and God and Christ Jesus who give us total peace. God and Christ Jesus show grace when they love us even though we do not deserve that love. God and Christ Jesus give peace, or total well\-being, because they have fully restored us to God. Paul reminds the Philippians that God acts as a loving father to the Philippians when Paul calls God "the father." And Paul reminds the Philippians that Jesus is the Savior that God promised would save us and bring us this grace and peace when Paul calls Jesus "Christ."

Stop here and discuss as a group what kinds of greetings you give each other as Christians in your community. How will you translate this blessing of "grace and peace" in your translation? Pause this audio here.

In the second part of the message, Paul thanks the Philippians for their partnership in the gospel. The Philippians are partners with Paul in the work because the Philippians have agreed to work together with Paul to share the good news about Jesus, to encourage and pray for Paul, and to give money to help the mission. The Philippians have partnered with Paul "from the first day," or from the time when the Philippians first met Paul and believed in Jesus.

Stop here and discuss as a group: Tell a story about a time when people worked together to do something important. How did you describe how those people worked together for a good thing? How will you talk about the Philippians' "partnership in the gospel, or good news?" Pause this audio here.

Paul loves the Philippians, and Paul is very confident that the good work God started when God saved the Philippians, God will finish! The Philippians will continue to follow Jesus, to know Jesus and become more like Jesus, and to share Jesus with others. The Philippians will continue to do this until the "day of Christ Jesus." When Paul and other Christians talked about the "day of Christ Jesus," people understood that Jesus is coming back again someday. On this day, Jesus will reward his followers and defeat everything that is evil. Paul and the early Christians were aware that Jesus will come back one day, and the Christians lived their lives expecting that day to come.

Stop here and discuss as a group how you will talk about the fact that Paul is confident that God will finish the work God started in the lives of the Philippian Christians. God will continue to work in the Christians' lives until Jesus comes back again! Pause this audio here.

Paul explains why he loves the Philippians so much. Paul says that he has the Philippians "in his heart," or that Paul loves the Philippians and thinks about the Philippians with joy. In Paul's culture, people described where they felt love and compassion as being in their heart.

Stop here and tell a story as a group about someone you love very much or are happy to know. How do you talk about your joy in being with that person? How will you say that Paul has the Philippians "in his heart?" Pause this audio here.

Paul continues to say that the Philippian Christians share in God's grace. In other words, the Philippian Christians have also chosen to follow Jesus, and God has given the Philippian Christians the gift of grace by loving them and forgiving them even though they did not deserve it. This gift of grace means that the Philippian Christians love Paul and work with him even though Paul is "in chains," or in prison. While Paul was first in prison in Rome, guards probably guarded Paul in a house. The guards probably chained Paul to a guard so that Paul would not escape. This is why Paul is "in chains."

Paul has defended, or explained, why he trusts in Jesus, and Paul has confirmed, or persuaded people, that Jesus is the promised Savior. That is why Paul is in prison. The Philippians share in Paul's suffering, either because the Philippian Christians support Paul while he is in prison, or because other people also persecute the Philippians because they follow Jesus.

Paul says that he wishes to be with the Philippian Christians and to experience their friendship again—he loves the Philippians with the same kind of love that Jesus has for them. Paul says, "God is my witness," to show how sincere and serious Paul is when he says that Paul loves the Philippians.

In the final part of this passage, Paul prays that the Philippians would focus on what really matters—that the Philippians would love and value God and love and value other people more and more. Paul prays that this deep love would come from the Philippians' knowledge of who God is—that the Philippians would really know God. Paul prays that the Philippians would also love others because the Philippians have insight, or an ability to understand what is good and right. Then the Philippians can decide how to act in the best way. The Philippians can be pure. In other words, the Philippians are not divided between evil and good—the Philippians fully follow God. Then the Philippians can be blameless, or not guilty, in front of Christ on the day when Christ Jesus comes back to judge everyone in the world. When people are pure and blameless, those people are righteous—they are in good relationship with God. This righteousness is like a wonderful fruit that only Jesus can make happen in our lives.

Stop here and, as a group, discuss how you can show that God fills people with knowledge of who God is, and with an ability to understand what is good and right. What do people like this do? How will you describe these people? How will you talk about these people being righteous, or in a right and good relationship with God? Pause this audio here.

Stop here and discuss as a group how you will talk about the fact that righteousness comes "through" Christ Jesus. How is it possible for Jesus to be the one who gives us righteousness? How will you say that Jesus fills us with the fruit of righteousness? Pause this audio here.

When we have this fruit called righteousness, we have a good relationship with God. When we have this good relationship with God, we have God's love for each other. This is good news! And this good news makes us want to give glory and praise to God! When we give glory to God, we realize that God is majestic and wonderful.

Now the group should storyboard, draw, or use objects to visualize the passage and the action in it.

Filling the Gaps
----------------

Listen to an audio version of Philippians 1:1—11 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In this step, the group will discuss key terms and words in this passage.

Paul and Timothy call themselves servants or slaves of Christ Jesus. Slaves did not have any choice but to work for their master. Paul and Timothy call themselves slaves because Paul and Timothy always work for God and belong to God. Use a term that shows that Paul and Timothy belong to God, but make sure the term does not communicate that God is a harsh master. Christ is the same as the Messiah. Christ is the title for the King and Savior that God promised would come to save God's people.

Pause the audio here and talk about how you will say that Paul and Timothy are **slaves** of **Christ** Jesus. If you have already translated this idea in another book of the Bible, use the same words or phrases that you have used there. For more information about slave and Christ, refer to the Master Glossary. Pause this audio here.

Paul writes to the "saints," or holy people, in Philippi. Saints or holy people describe those who believe that Jesus is the promised Savior. These "holy people" are people whom God chose and set apart to belong to God.

Stop here and discuss what word or description you will use for **saint**, or holy people. For more information about "saint," refer to the Master Glossary. Use the same word for saint that you have used in translations of other books. Pause this audio here.

Paul is especially talking to the overseers and the deacons of the church in Philippi. Overseers are church leaders, who are like shepherds taking care of sheep. Overseers are responsible to make sure the people in the church have everything they need spiritually. This means overseers help the people understand the good news about Jesus and how to live for Jesus. The deacons are leaders in the church who assist the overseer. The deacons are responsible to make sure the people in the church have everything they need physically.

Stop here and discuss what word or description you will use for **overseers** and **deacons**. For more information about overseers and deacons, refer to the Master Glossary. Pause this audio here.

Paul asks God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ to give God's saints in Philippi grace and peace. A "lord" is a master or the person who is in control of other people. When Paul calls Jesus "Lord," Paul is saying that Jesus has the same authority as God has.

If necessary, stop here and discuss what word you will use for **lord**. For more information about lord, refer to the Master Glossary. Use the same word for lord that you have used in other translations. Pause this audio here.

When God shows grace to someone, God is showing his favor and kindness to people who do not deserve that favor and kindness. When someone has peace, it means that person has a good relationship with God that is full of life.

Stop here and discuss what words you will use for **grace** and **peace**. Use the same words for grace and peace that you have used in previous translations. For more information about grace and peace, refer to the Master Glossary. Pause this audio here.

"Father" refers to our heavenly Father, who is God. Paul uses the same word for Father that you would use for your own father.

If necessary, stop here and discuss how you will talk about God as a **Father** in this book. Use the same word for God as Father that you have used in other books of the Bible. Pause this audio here.

Paul prays and thanks God for the Philippians' partnership in the gospel. When someone prays to God, that person talks with God. This kind of prayer is when someone very specifically asks God for help. The gospel is the good news that Jesus died, rose again, and is with God in heaven. Jesus did this so that Jesus could save us and restore our relationship with God when we trust in Jesus.

Stop here and discuss as a group what words you will use for **pray** and **gospel**. Remember that this word for prayer specifically means to ask for help from God. For more information about pray and gospel, refer to the Master Glossary. Use the same words or phrases as you have used in previous translations. Pause this audio here.

Paul has the Philippians "in his heart," meaning that Paul has affection for the Philippians and is joyful when Paul thinks about the Philippians. In Paul's culture, people talked about the heart as special language for the place where people's thoughts and emotions happened.

Stop here and discuss as a group how you will translate that Paul had the Philippians in Paul's **heart**. For more information about heart, refer to the Master Glossary. If you use the word for heart, use the same word for heart that you have used in previous translations. Pause this audio here.

Paul says that he is in chains. This means that Paul is in prison, but in that time, guards usually chained a prisoner to a guard so the prisoner could not escape. These "chains" may have been chains that people made from strong rope tied together, or they may have been metal chains.

Stop here and discuss as a group what word or phrase you will use for **chains**. Remember that Paul is using the idea of chains to say that Paul is a captive and that guards guard Paul all the time. Pause this audio here.

God is a witness, or "testifies," that Paul wishes to be with the Philippians. In other words, God has seen and knows how Paul feels about the Philippians.

Stop here and discuss as a group how you will translate that God **testifies** how Paul longs for, or wishes, to see the Philippians again. Pause this audio here.

Paul prays that the Philippians would live pure and blameless lives and that God would fill the Philippians with righteousness. If someone is pure, it means that person is clearly thinking about God—that person knows what is right and they follow it. When someone is blameless, it means nobody may accuse that person because that person has not done anything wrong. Also, if God fills a person with righteousness, it means that person has a right relationship with God, because that person has trusted in Christ Jesus. Paul tells the Philippians that Jesus is the one who fills their life with righteousness because Jesus always did the right thing, and Jesus gives us the strength to follow Jesus.

Stop here and discuss as a group how you will translate **pure**, **blameless**, and **righteousness**. For more information about pure, blameless, and righteousness, refer to the Master Glossary. If you have translated these ideas in other parts of the Bible, use the same words here. Pause this audio here.

Paul says that this will bring "glory and praise to God." Glory has the idea of weight, or that something is very important. God's glory is God's majesty and importance.

Stop here and discuss as a group how you will translate "This will bring much **glory** and **praise** to God." Glory and praise are in the Master Glossary.

Setting the Stage
-----------------

Listen to an audio version of Philippians 1:12—18a in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

Paul started this letter with Paul's normal introductions and prayer. Paul talked about how much Paul loves the Philippians and how much Paul wants the Philippians to love and know God more and love other people more and more.Paul starts this part of the letter in the same way that Paul usually starts talking about a new topic. Paul calls his audience, Paul's fellow believers, "brothers." Paul tells his fellow believers that Paul wants them to know something. Paul calls the believers brothers because God's people talked about themselves as if they were a family. God's people talked about God as their Father who adopted them, and because God adopted all of them, God's people talked about each other as if they were brothers and sisters. Even though Paul just calls the Philippians "brothers," Paul is actually talking to both the men and the women in the city of Philippi.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, when do people talk about someone as family even if they are not related to that person by blood? If you do talk about someone you are really close to as family, when do you do it? If you do not talk about people in this way, how do you talk about them? Pause this audio here.

The Philippians would know that Paul was in prison in Rome. Paul tells the Philippian Christians that Jesus' good news spread further because of what had happened to Paul when the authorities put Paul in prison in Rome. Rome was the capital city of the Roman Empire. In those days, the leader, or emperor, of the Roman Empire ruled over all the countries that Paul had visited. Paul's prison was probably actually a house where Paul lived and where people could visit Paul. You will remember that the guards probably chained Paul to a soldier at all times so that Paul would not escape. This gave Paul constant ability to tell the good news about Jesus to whatever guard the soldiers had chained Paul to!

Stop here and, as a group, look at a map of the Roman Empire that includes the city of Rome. Pause this audio here.

Paul says that what happened to Paul advanced the gospel, or the good news about Jesus. In other words, more people heard about who Jesus was and what Jesus did. Paul probably also means that more people trust Jesus because of what happened to Paul. People would not normally expect that the good news about Jesus would spread so much when Paul was in prison. The palace guard were the soldiers who guarded the leader, or emperor, of Rome. These soldiers sometimes also guarded other important officials. Setting the Stage
-----------------

Listen to an audio version of Philippians 4:10—23 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In the previous passages, Paul told the Christians in Philippi some final things Paul wanted them to do. In this passage Paul thanks the Christians in Philippi for the help they have given Paul. Paul also finishes his letter to the Christians in Philippi.

First, Paul tells the Christians in Philippi that Paul has joy because of Paul's relationship with the Lord. Paul is rejoicing in the Lord greatly because the Christians in Philippi have shown again that they are concerned for Paul. Paul clarifies that the Christians in Philippi were concerned for Paul before, but the Philippian Christians did not have an opportunity to show their concern because Paul was in a different city than Philippi. Now, Paul is talking about the fact that the Christians in Philippi sent Paul a gift while Paul was in prison. You will remember that Paul was a prisoner in a house in Rome because Paul taught about Jesus. The prison guards did not give the prisoners clothes or food or anything else. Instead, the people who cared about the prisoner would give the prisoner what they needed. So the Christians in Philippi showed that they cared about Paul because they sent a gift to Paul to help Paul survive while he was in prison.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, how do people punish people who commit a crime? How do people take care of people who are in prison? Pause this audio here.

Paul also clarifies that Paul does not need the Philippians to give Paul anything, because Paul has learned to be content with whatever Paul has. Paul knows both how to be content when Paul is poor and when Paul has more than Paul needs. In everything and in every way, God has taught Paul deep and important things that helped Paul understand more things about God. In Paul's culture, people often formed secret groups. When people joined the secret groups, the members in the group welcomed new people by telling those people the secrets that only the people in the group knew. Paul talks about how God shared information with Paul in a similar way. God's information is not secret because God gives it to all his people, but God's information is special and helpful to God's people, and only God's people really understand what God teaches them!

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, how do people learn things that they did not know before? Are there special events that take place to show that someone has new knowledge or belongs now to a special group of people? Pause this audio here.

God has taught Paul how to be content when Paul has plenty of food, how to be content when Paul is hungry, how to be content when Paul has everything Paul needs, and how to be content when Paul must struggle to meet Paul's needs. Paul can be content through Christ, because Christ gives Paul strength. Despite Paul not needing the gift, the Christians in Philippi were kind to help Paul so that Paul had less distress.

Then Paul reminds the Philippian Christians of how they helped Paul. The Philippian Christians already know this part of the story, but Paul reminds them again that the church in Philippi was the only church that helped Paul when Paul left Macedonia, right after the Philippian Christians first started following Jesus. Macedonia was the region that the city of Philippi was located in. In fact, no church gave anything to Paul, and Paul did not receive anything from anyone except the Christians in Philippi. Even when Paul was in the city of Thessalonica near Philippi, the Christians in Philippi sent Paul some help more than once.

Stop here and, as a group, look at a map of Macedonia, Philippi, and Thessalonica. Pause this audio here.

Paul is not trying to get a gift from the Christians in Philippi. Instead, Paul wants the believers to do good things for a different reason. Paul compares the good things Philippian believers are doing to help Paul preach the good news to adding money to a bank account. In that time, people put their money or valuable things in a bank, which was a person or organization that takes care of and loans valuable things like money. A bank also paid people a little money so that the bank could use the money for loans or other things. So Paul is talking about the gift that the Christians in Philippi gave Paul as if it were money that the Christians put in a bank with God, and God will give the Christians in Philippi rewards for the money they gave.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, where do people put their money or valuable things? How can people earn money with the money they put in a bank? Pause this audio here.

Paul has received everything Paul needs and more. Paul is fully satisfied since Epaphroditus brought the gifts that the Christians in Philippi sent to Paul. Paul calls this gift a fragrant offering, a sacrifice that God accepts and is pleased with. Paul compares the gift that the believers in Philippi gave to an animal that people sacrifice to God. Long ago, God gave the Israelite people instructions on how to bring animals as a gift to honor God. When the Israelites offered these animals as a sacrifice in the way God instructed, the Israelites pleased God and God accepted the sacrifice. In the same way, God is pleased when the Philippian believers give a gift to help people like Paul.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, what do people sacrifice to a god? How do people make these sacrifices? Pause this audio here.

Because the Christians in Philippi helped Paul, Paul's God will fully satisfy every need that the Christians in Philippi have. God will satisfy these needs from the glorious riches that God gives through Christ Jesus. Then, Paul glorifies, or praises, God, who is also our Father, forever.

Stop here and discuss as a group about a time when you have seen God provide many good things for God's people who follow Christ Jesus. Pause this audio here.

Finally, Paul tells the Christians in Philippi to greet every saint, or holy person in Christ Jesus who is in Philippi. You will remember that every Christian who trusts in Christ Jesus is holy, or set apart, for God. These people are saints. Paul often ended his letters with greetings like this.

The brothers and sisters who are with Paul in Rome send a greeting to the Christians in Philippi. The rest of the saints, or holy people, in Rome also greet the Christians in Philippi. Even the people or servants in Caesar's household who work for Caesar greet the Christians in Philippi. Caesar is the title for the Roman emperor. These people in Caesar's household had probably become Christians, and that is why these people sent greetings to the Philippian Christians.

Finally, Paul gives the Christians in Philippi a blessing: "May the Lord Jesus Christ be kind and loving to you all."

Defining the Scenes
-------------------

Listen to an audio version of Philippians 4:10—23 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In this step, the group will define the scenes, characters, and setting of this passage. Then the group will visualize this passage.

This passage has three parts.

**In the first part:** Paul rejoices because of the way the Philippians cared for Paul and explains how Christ causes Paul to be content in any circumstance.

**In the second part:** Paul thanks the Philippians for their gift, especially since the Philippians are the only group that helped when Paul needed it. Paul is certain that God will reward the Philippians' generosity and will provide what the Philippians need.

**In the third part:** Paul finishes his letter to the Christians in Philippi.

The characters in this passage are:

* Paul
* God
* The Christians in Philippi
* People in churches
* Epaphroditus
* The Lord Jesus Christ
* The holy people or saints, probably in Rome
* The brothers and sisters with Paul
* And the saints in Caesar's household

As a group, pay attention to these parts of the passage's setting:

This is the last passage in Paul's letter to the Philippians. In this passage, Paul thanks the Christians in Philippi for the help the Christians have given Paul, and Paul tells the Christians in Philippi that God will reward them for being kind. Paul also greets the Christians in Philippi on different people's behalf.

In the first part, Paul tells the Christians in Philippi that Paul is rejoicing in the Lord greatly. When Paul says that Paul is rejoicing in the Lord, Paul is using special language to say that Paul is happy because Paul has a relationship with the Lord. In this case, Paul is probably referring to Jesus as "Lord," or master.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: How will you talk about how Paul rejoices because Paul has a good relationship with the Lord? Pause this audio here.

Paul is rejoicing in the Lord greatly because the Christians in Philippi have finally shown concern again for Paul. So that Paul does not sound like he is criticizing the Philippians, Paul clarifies that he knows that the believers were concerned for Paul before, but they did not have an opportunity to show their concern. Paul is talking about the fact that the Christians in Philippi sent Paul a gift while Paul was in prison.

Even though Paul is thankful for the gift, Paul explains that Paul is content no matter what happens. When someone is content, that person does not need anything else. Paul is not talking about this gift because Paul needs more and wants the believers to give again. Instead, Paul has learned to be content when Paul has very little money, and Paul has learned to be content when Paul has a lot of valuable things. In everything and in every way, God has taught Paul deep and important things. God's information is special and very important to God's people, and only God's people really understand the things God teaches them. In this way, God has taught Paul the "secret" of being content in every situation—Paul has all that Paul needs!

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: What is the best way to translate when Paul says that God has taught Paul the "secret" of being content in every situation? Pause this audio here.

Paul talks about what God has taught Paul with two sets of opposite phrases. Both of these phrases express the same idea with different words. First, God has taught Paul how to be content when Paul has plenty of food or when Paul is hungry. Second, God has taught Paul how to be content when Paul has plenty of valuable things or when Paul struggles to meet Paul's needs.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, how do people talk about being rich versus being poor? How will you talk about having a lot of food versus being hungry? How will you talk about having plenty of valuable things versus struggling to get what you need? Pause this audio here.

Paul can do all these things, or be content, through the one who strengthens Paul. When Paul talks about the "one," Paul is talking about Christ, but Paul does not actually use the name Christ. Paul probably means that Paul can be content in all of these situations because Christ helps Paul. Christ causes Paul to be strong in every kind of circumstance.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: How will you say that Paul says that he can do all these things through Christ who gives Paul strength? Pause this audio here.

Despite Paul not needing the gift, the Christians in Philippi were kind to share Paul's distress. When Paul says that the Christians in Philippi share his distress, Paul is using special language to mean that the Philippians helped Paul so that Paul had less distress.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: When did someone help you when you were experiencing a difficult time? How do you describe the way that the other person helped you when you were in trouble? What is the best way to translate when Paul said that the Christians in Philippi share Paul's distress? Pause this audio here.

Paul continues to talk about the way that the Christians in Philippi have helped Paul. The Philippians know this story, so Paul is probably reminding the Philippians of this to praise and encourage the Philippians. When the Philippians had first heard the gospel, the Philippians helped Paul when Paul traveled through Macedonia. When Paul talks about the early days of the gospel, Paul means that Paul had not shared the gospel in very many places yet, and not very many people in Philippi had started to follow Jesus. In fact, no church joined giving and receiving with Paul except the Christians in Philippi. When Paul talks about giving and receiving, Paul is probably talking about churches or groups of Christians giving money to Paul, and Paul receiving that money from them. Paul might also be talking about how Paul gave by teaching the people about Jesus, and then the people gave back by giving Paul money. Even when Paul was in the city of Thessalonica, the Christians in Philippi sent Paul some help more than once.

Paul is not trying to get a gift from the Christians in Philippi. Instead, Paul wants the believers to receive something! Paul uses business terms to talk about what happens when the believers give. When Paul talks about the profit in their account, Paul is using special language. In Paul's culture, people often used organizations called banks to hold their money or things that are valuable. The bank also paid people a little money so that the bank could use the money for loans or other things. So Paul is talking about the gift that the Christians in Philippi gave Paul as if it were money that they put in a bank with God, and God will give the Christians in Philippi rewards for the money they gave. When the Philippian Christians do things for God, it is like they are adding to their bank account with God. Paul also uses this special language to emphasize that the Philippians do not owe Paul anything; Paul has received everything that the Philippians owed Paul.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: How do you talk about financial matters? When would you compare something like a gift to interest on an account? What is the best way to translate when Paul says he wants the Christians in Philippi to make a profit in their account? Pause this audio here.

Paul has received everything Paul needs and more. Paul is fully satisfied since Epaphroditus brought the gifts that the Christians in Philippi sent to Paul. Paul compares this gift to a fragrant offering, a sacrifice that God accepts and is pleased with. Paul is using special language by talking as if the gift the Christians in Philippi gave was an animal they sacrificed to God. God often talked about animals that people sacrificed as acceptable and pleasing to God.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: The Old Testament describes in Exodus 29 how the Israelites were supposed to give sacrifices. In Exodus 29:38—41, the storyteller describes a sacrifice that pleases God. If you have already translated this passage, how could you use similar language here? What is the best way to translate when Paul calls the gift a fragrant offering? Pause this audio here.

Because the Christians in Philippi helped Paul, Paul's God will fully satisfy every need that the Christians in Philippi have. God will satisfy these needs from the "glorious," or great, riches that God gives through Jesus Christ. Paul might also mean that God has this great wealth because of what Jesus Christ has done. Then, Paul wishes for people to glorify the God and Father of Paul and the Christians in Philippi forever and ever. Paul is both praising God and urging others to praise God too. When Paul says forever and ever, Paul is using special language to mean that Paul wants people to glorify, or praise, God forever. People often said things like forever and ever to emphasize something that is certain and true, and that never ends.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: How do you praise God? How do you urge others to praise God? How do you talk about things that last forever or should last forever? What is the best way to translate when Paul says that he wants people to glorify God forever and ever? Pause this audio here.

Paul ends this part by saying "amen," which means that Paul wants this prayer to be true.

Stop here and discuss as a group: How do you finish a prayer and say that you want that prayer to be true? How will you say "amen" here? Pause this audio here.

In the second part, Paul tells the Christians in Philippi to greet every holy person in Christ Jesus. The holy people, or saints, are people who obey Jesus and have a close relationship with Jesus. When Paul talks about people as being "in Christ," Paul means that they belong to Christ. Paul is telling the Christians in Philippi to greet the other believers they meet on Paul's behalf.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, how do people send greetings to people they can not talk to personally? What words do people use to describe the greetings? How will you talk about Paul telling the Christians in Philippi to greet the holy people, or "saints," in Christ Jesus? Pause this audio here.

Paul also sends a greeting to the Christians in Philippi from three groups of people. First, the brothers and sisters with Paul send a greeting to the Christians in Philippi. Some people think these brothers and sisters were the people directly helping Paul while Paul was in prison. Second, the other holy people, or saints, also greet the Christians in Philippi. Paul is probably talking about the other believers in Rome, not all the holy people in the whole world. Third, the people in Caesar's household also greet the Christians in Philippi. Caesar was the emperor, or the person in charge of the entire Roman Empire, which included Philippi. The people in Caesar's household were probably servants who worked for Caesar.

Finally, Paul gives the Christians in Philippi a blessing: "May the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit." In other words, Paul wants the Lord Jesus Christ to love the Christians in Philippi even though they do not deserve it. When Paul mentions the Christians' spirit, Paul is using special language to mean the Christians in Philippi themselves. In Paul's culture, people believed that people had a spirit, or soul, that was part of who they were and that would live after they died.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: What kinds of blessings do people give each other in your culture? What specific blessings do people use for the end of a message? What kinds of words do you use in these blessings? What is the best way to translate when Paul asks for the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ to be with the spirits of the Christians in Philippi? In this passage, Paul calls Jesus his **Lord**. A Lord is someone who is a master over other people, who has full authority, and who deserves for people to give him respect. When you address someone as "Lord," you are saying that person has authority over you. Use the same word or phrase for Lord as you used in previous passages. For more information on Lord, refer to the Master Glossary.

The Christians in Philippi helped Paul in the early days of the **gospel**, or good news. The word gospel is a translation of a Greek word that literally means "good news." The good news in the Bible is that God has made a way to save people. In the New Testament, it becomes clear that the way that God saves his people is through Jesus's death and resurrection. Use the same word or phrase for gospel as you used in previous passages. For more information on gospel, refer to the Master Glossary.

None of the other **churches** supported Paul. In the Bible, the church is a community of people who believe in Jesus. Sometimes people use the word "church" to refer to a group of believers in one specific place, and sometimes the word means all the people on earth together who believe in Jesus. Here Paul is talking about the local churches in various cities. Use the same word or phrase for church as you used in previous passages. For more information on church, refer to the Master Glossary.

Paul calls the gift that the Christians in Philippi gave him a **sacrifice**. A sacrifice is a gift to God. People often placed the gift on a platform made of earth or large stones, which people called an altar. Use the same word or phrase for sacrifice as you used in previous passages. For more information on sacrifice, refer to the Master Glossary.

Because the Christians in Philippi helped Paul, God will help those Christians from his **glorious** riches. When God shows his glory, people can see how beautiful, powerful, and majestic God is. Here, the riches that God has are part of how impressive God is. Use the same word or phrase for glory as you used in previous passages. For more information on glory, refer to the Master Glossary.

The glorious riches that God has will be in **Christ** Jesus. The word "Christ" refers to the special King and Savior that God had promised to send. Use the same word or phrase for Christ as you used in previous passages. For more information on Christ, refer to the Master Glossary.

Paul tells the believers to **glorify** the believers' God and Father forever. When people glorify God, people praise God, admire God, and say good things about God. Use the same word or phrase for glory as you used earlier in this passage and in previous passages. For more information on glory, refer to the Master Glossary.

After Paul tells the believers to glorify God, Paul says, "Amen." When someone offers a prayer to God, you can say "Amen." This means that you hope and believe that the prayer will be answered.

The **brothers** greet the Christians in Philippi. Paul often calls Christians "brothers" or "brothers and sisters" because Christians are like members of the same family with God as their father. Use the same word or phrase for brothers as you used in previous passages.

If the family had servants or slaves, then the family included the servants in the household as well. In this passage, the members of Caesar's household who became Christians are probably servants.

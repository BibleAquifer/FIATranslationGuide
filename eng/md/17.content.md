Setting the Stage
-----------------

Listen to an audio version of Esther 1:1—9 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

\[The book of Esther is a story about events that happened 60 to 70 years after the first Jews went back to Jerusalem after the exile. The Jews in Jerusalem had completed rebuilding the temple about 40 years before this story. This story happens around 30 years before Ezra, and then Nehemiah, returned to Jerusalem, and around 500 years before Jesus lived on earth. During this story, many Jews still live in Persia, far from Jerusalem. Many of these Jews have settled in Persia and do not return to Jerusalem.]{.mark}

The story begins at the time of a king called "Ahasuerus." People also call this king "Xerxes." These are two names for the same king. King Xerxes is a powerful king of Persia who rules over a very large kingdom. King Xerxes' kingdom reaches from India to Cush in northern Africa. The king divides the kingdom into 127 smaller parts, which he calls provinces, and each province has its own leaders who obey the king.

Stop here and, as a group, look at a map that shows Persia and the stronghold of Susa. Talk about how large King Xerxes's kingdom is. Pause this audio here.

King Xerxes sits on his royal throne in the city of Susa, the capital city of Persia. Susa has a stronghold. A stronghold is a strong, raised place that people build to keep the king safe. A stronghold is on high ground above the city. From this place, people can see the whole city below. The king's palace is inside this stronghold.

Stop here and, as a group, look at a picture of what the stronghold of Susa may have looked like. Pause this audio here.

At around 483 B.C., in the third year that Xerxes is king, Xerxes gives a great banquet for his officials and servants. A banquet is a large meal with many people who eat and drink together for a special reason. King Xerxes invites the governors of Persia and Media. The Persians and the Medes are two groups of people in the king's kingdom. King Xerxes rules over both groups. King Xerxes also invites military leaders and important men from each province.

During this banquet, King Xerxes shows his great wealth and the riches and beauty of Xerxes' kingdom. Xerxes continues the banquet for 180 days. In that culture, a king often shows wealth and power in public ways because the king wants people to see his greatness and honor the king.

Stop here and discuss these questions as a group: In your culture, how do leaders show they are wealthy and powerful? When a leader does this, what might people in the community think or say? Pause this audio here.

After this banquet ends, King Xerxes gives another banquet. This banquet is for all the people in the city of Susa. King Xerxes invites both important people and less important people.

Stop here and discuss as a group: Who might attend a large banquet in your culture? What happens if both important people and common people come together? Pause this audio here.

This banquet lasts for seven days. The king does this banquet in a courtyard near the king's palace. A courtyard is an open space with no roof and a stone floor. Gardens with trees and plants are around this space. These gardens are for beauty, not for farming. A strong wall surrounds the whole area. The palace near the courtyard here may be an open building with a roof and columns, but no walls.

The garden has white and blue cloth, perhaps to shade the guests from the sun. Servants tie the cloth with cords of fine linen and purple cloth. Purple cloth is very expensive, so it shows that the king is wealthy and important. The garden has silver rings and marble pillars. Servants use the rings to tie back the cloth with cords of fine linen and purple cloth. Workers made the couches from gold and silver. People lie on these couches as they eat their food at low tables. The floor has marble and other valuable stones like porphyry and mother\-of\-pearl. Porphyry is a hard, red or purple stone, and mother\-of\-pearl is the shiny white part inside a shell. Workers place these stones in patterns to make the floor look beautiful. People call this kind of floor a mosaic. All these things show how rich and powerful the king is.

Stop here and, as a group, look at a picture of a mosaic floor. Pause this audio here.

Stop here and discuss this question as a group: In your culture, what things show that a person is rich or important? What special materials or colors show that someone is important or wealthy? Pause this audio here.

Servants give drinks to the guests in golden goblets, or special cups. Each cup is different. The king offers his guests plenty of wine, a drink that people make from grapes. Royal wine means wine that belongs to the king. The king tells the servants to give each person as much as that person wants. In that culture, a king can show kindness and generosity by giving much food and drink.

Stop here and, as a group, look at a picture of some different golden goblets. Talk about what you see. Pause this audio here.

At other times, the king may set rules for how much people should drink. But at this banquet, the king chooses not to use these rules. Instead, the king tells his servants to give each person what that person wants. Each guest can drink as much or as little as that person chooses.

At the same time, Queen Vashti also gives a separate banquet for the women. Queen Vashti holds this banquet elsewhere in the royal palace. The storyteller does not explain why Queen Vashti gives a separate banquet. In Persian royal households, the king often has a harem. A harem is a group of women connected to the king, including wives and concubines. These women often live in a separate part of the palace. Some scholars think that the women at this banquet may come from the royal household, possibly from the harem. But the storyteller does not say this clearly.

This part of the story shows King Xerxes' great power and wealth. The king wants people to see how great Xerxes' kingdom is and to honor Xerxes. In the next part of the story, we will learn what happens when the king gives Queen Vashti a command during the banquet.

Defining the Scenes
-------------------

Listen to an audio version of Esther 1:1—9 in the easiest\-to\-understand translation.

Pause this audio here.

In this step, the group will define the scenes, characters, and setting of this passage. Then the group will visualize this passage.

This passage has four scenes.

**In the first scene:** The storyteller introduces the story. The storyteller tells about King Xerxes and when Xerxes rules. The storyteller shows how large the kingdom is and explains that the king rules over many provinces.

**In the second scene:** King Xerxes gives a great banquet for his officials and leaders. During this banquet, the king shows his great wealth and the riches of his kingdom. The king continues this banquet for 180 days.

**In the third scene:** After this banquet, King Xerxes gives another banquet. This banquet is for all the people in the city of Susa. The king invites both important people and less important people. This banquet takes place in the courtyard near the palace and lasts for seven days.

**In the fourth scene:** At the same time, Queen Vashti gives a banquet for the women in another place in the royal palace.

The characters in this passage are:

* King Ahasuerus, who people also know as King Xerxes
* Officials and governors of Persia and Media
* Military leaders
* Nobles, or important men from the provinces
* Stewards
* People from the city of Susa
* Queen Vashti
* And the women at Queen Vashti's banquet

As a group, pay attention to these parts of the passage's setting:

The book begins in a way that people often used in that time to tell a true story. The storyteller tells when and where the story happens. The storyteller also introduces important people, including King Xerxes and Queen Vashti. This helps listeners to understand the setting and prepares them for the story. This part of the story also shows that the king is very wealthy and powerful. These ideas will appear again later in the story.

Stop here and discuss as a group: When people tell true stories in your culture, how do those people begin? What information do people give at the start of a story? Pause this audio here.

It is important to remember that King Xerxes rules over a very large kingdom. The king is in the city of Susa at this time, and the king sits on his throne in the city of Susa. When a king sits on his throne, it shows that the king has power and authority.

Susa has a stronghold, a special place where the king lives. Think about how you can show a great king who rules over many lands.

Susa is one of the king's main cities. Susa is lower in the land and warmer, so the king stays there during the colder part of the year. At other times, the king rules from other capital cities.

In the second scene, King Xerxes gathers his governors, officials, military leaders, and nobles, or important men. The king gives a banquet where he offers plenty of food and drink. The king shows his great wealth and the riches of his kingdom. The king continues this banquet over many days, for 180 days. The king wants people to see how great and powerful the king is.

Think about how you can show the king displaying his wealth. You could use simple objects to show valuable things. You could show many people coming to see the king, the king's palace, and what the king owns.

In the third scene, after this long time, King Xerxes gives another banquet. The storyteller uses the phrase "when these days are over" to show that one part of the story has finished and a new part begins.

Stop here and discuss as a group: How will you show that one part of the story has ended and a new part begins? How will you move from the first banquet to the second banquet? Pause this audio here.

This time, the king invites all the people in the city of Susa. The king invites both important people and less important people. This banquet lasts for seven days. The storyteller says that people come from the greatest to the least. This means people who are both the most important people and people who are the least important people. The phrase "from the greatest to the least" can also mean that all the people in the city come. At that time, large public banquets like this often included men and women. The storyteller later tells us that Queen Vashti holds a banquet for the women in another place.

Stop here and discuss as a group: How will you say "from the greatest to the least" in your language? What words will you use to show that all kinds of people are included?

Pause this audio here.

The king holds this banquet in a courtyard near the palace. The courtyard is an open space. Fine cloth hangs around the space, and pillars hold up the cloth. Servants tie the cloth to the pillars with cords and silver rings. We do not know exactly how the cloth hangs in the courtyard, but people attach the cloth to the pillars. People have made this cloth from fine linen and purple cloth. Linen is a strong, smooth cloth that people make from plant fibers. Purple cloth is very expensive and shows wealth. People make couches with frames of gold and silver. People place soft mats or cushions on top of the couches. People lie on these couches as they eat their food at low tables. The floor has many kinds of stone. Workers place the stones in patterns to make the floor look beautiful. People call this kind of floor a mosaic. Think about how you can show this place. You could mark out a large open space. You could place objects to show couches, pillars, and decorations.

Stop here and look as a group at pictures of mosaics, pillars, and couches like these. Talk about what you see. Pause this audio here.

Stop here and discuss as a group: How will you describe this courtyard in your language?

What words will you use for the cloth, the pillars, and the couches? How will you show that this place looks rich and beautiful? Pause this audio here.

During this banquet, servants serve drinks in different types of gold goblets or cups.

Stop here and, as a group, look at some pictures of different kinds of gold goblets or cups. Talk about what you see. What words would you use for goblets or drinking cups like these in your language? Pause this audio here.

The king gives much wine to the people. You will remember that wine is a drink that people make from grapes. The king tells the stewards to give each person as much wine as that person wants. A steward is a servant with a more important role than the other servants. This person manages the work in the household and tells the other servants what to do. At other times, the king may make rules for how much people can drink. But in this scene, the king does not use these rules. Each person chooses how much to drink. Think about how you can show the servants serving the people their drinks. Think about how you can show people choosing how much to drink. Notice that the king gives food and drink freely. This shows that the king is very generous and very wealthy.

At the same time, in the fourth scene, Queen Vashti gives a banquet for the women. Queen Vashti holds this banquet in another place in the royal palace. The storyteller does not explain why Queen Vashti gives a separate banquet. Think about how you can show two banquets happening at the same time in different places.

Think about how one part of the story leads to the next part. This story moves from a long display of the king's wealth to a large banquet for the people and then to two banquets happening at the same time. The king shows his power and wealth to many people. This prepares us for what will happen next in the story.

Stop here and discuss as a group: What do you learn about King Xerxes from these scenes? What kind of leader does the king seem to be? Pause this audio here.

Now, the group should storyboard, draw, or use objects to visualize the passage and the action in it.

King Xerxes rules over a very large kingdom. The king rules over many lands and many people. The king sits on his royal throne in the city of Susa. * I feel free to choose how much wine I have to drink.

Continue the drama.

At the same time, Queen Vashti gives a banquet for the women. Queen Vashti holds this banquet in another place in the royal palace.

Pause the drama. Ask the person playing Queen Vashti, "What are you feeling or thinking?" The person might answer things like:

* I want to host this banquet well; or
* I want to honor these women; or
* I hope the women are enjoying my banquet.

Ask the person playing the women, "What are you feeling or thinking?" The person might answer things like:

* I am happy. It is good to be together; or
* The food and drink are delicious! or
* I wonder what is happening at the king's big banquet.

-----------------

Pause this audio here.

Pause this audio here.

Next, the group can decide whose version of the passage you like the most. You may decide that you liked the way one group member told one scene but you liked the way a different group member told another scene.

Pause this audio here.

Finally, the group can put together a final version of the passage that everyone agrees on. Each group member should practice telling this final version of the passage.
